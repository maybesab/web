00 Problem

Users think in their language, but they only trust AI in English.

We surveyed 24 multilingual researchers. Between them, participants spoke 14 languages.

  1. They avoid their language

    11 of 21

    rarely or never prompt AI in their native language

  2. It fails them

    11 of 24

    get worse answers in their native language

    Top challenge

  3. They want help in the tool

    12 of 24

    want better multilingual support inside the interface

    Top request

01 Research

Every breakdown was about meaning, not mechanics.

Contextual inquiry: 5 sessions in participants' own workspaces. Most ran 45 to 50 minutes; one ran about two hours.

One participant's real workflow broke across four apps

  1. 01GmailMentor emails an arXiv linkLanguage barriers cause stress
  2. 02arXivDownloads the PDF
  3. 03PreviewReads the abstractDoesn't understand some terms
  4. 04ChatGPTUploads the PDF, asks for an overviewConfused by hallucinations
  5. 05ChatGPTAsks about terms, reads in ChineseFriction switching languages

What we observed

  • Language mixing is deliberate.

    P1 drafts in Chinese, then puts key terms in English. P3 thinks in Hindi and English but types only English, because switching scripts is slow and he trusts English output more.

  • The AI invents terms.

    Worst in low-resource languages.

  • Dialects get mixed up.

    P4 asked for Macedonian and got Bulgarian or Serbian.

  • Tone comes back wrong.

    P2's Korean summaries read as stiff and artificial.

Part C · Rosetta v2 · designed solo in Figma

One workspace instead of four apps.

Finding that drove the change

Translation load didn't drop (p = .054), and the verification requirements were never built.

Layout: one job per pane

Paper · 30%

Shows where the answer came from

Scrolls to the passage the answer came from, highlighted and tagged “Cited in answer.”

Conversation · 45%

Every claim is traceable

Every claim carries a section and paragraph tag back to the paper. Highlight any answer to act on it.

Verify · 25%

There when needed

Collapsed to a thin rail until needed; the conversation grows into the free space. Source, Check (back-translate, flagged terms), History (versions, compare, restore).

Decision calloutv2 layout

Decision
Three panes, one job each: Paper 30%, Conversation 45%, Verify 25% collapsed to a thin rail.
Why
It replaces the Gmail, arXiv, Preview, ChatGPT hop, and keeps a dense screen legible with one job per pane.
Tradeoff
Checking sits one click away instead of always on screen, so the conversation keeps the space most of the time.

05 Outcomes

Faster on both tasks, easier on only one.

So what

Speed improved on both tasks, but translation relief was uneven. A faster translation still has to be checked for meaning, and v1 gave no way to check it. That is v2's job.

How v2 would be built and measured (planned)

Build

  • Gemini returns paired English and Burmese sentences as structured output, which enables aligned rows.
  • Language detection runs on output before render.
  • Terms are matched against the source passage, not the model's own confidence.
  • The generated prompt exposes assumptions as editable spans.

Measure

  • Rerun the same within-subjects study.
  • Add time-to-verify and error-catch rate.
  • Success: translation load drops significantly, not just time.

06 Reflection

Anyone acting on AI output needs to see what the model assumed.

  1. 1

    Numbers and research need each other.

    The study showed v1 was faster. The research showed why translation still felt like work. Neither alone would have found the gap v2 closes.

  2. 2

    Design interaction states before the build.

    Undo and system status were the top issues in testing. Designing states first would have caught them.

  3. 3

    Test verification, not just speed.

    We measured time and load. Trust needs its own measure, which v2 adds.
